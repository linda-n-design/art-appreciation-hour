Notes from the conversation

Six people attended the inaugural art appreciation hour on November 9, 2026.

Initial reactions to Remedios Varo’s Harmony include its richness of detail, not knowing what to focus on or how to approach it, and the feeling that the picture seems unsettled; the word “disharmony” was used.

We also talked about how the composition’s light directs the eye.

The light

What’s highlighted and where’s the light coming from? Conversely, what’s being recessed?

The primary light emanates from the chest of the left-side wall muse. This muse is collaborating with the picture’s central figure.

Light is also emitting from the top of the room, which lies outside the picture’s frame.

Even though there are windows in the room, they don’t bring in light.

Dis/harmony

The picture feels both disorderly and orderly.

The orderly aspect includes the patterns (e.g., two wall muses, two treble clef instruments, the square tile floor). The picture’s composition is almost symmetrical.

But the room seems to be unraveling, with the muses emerging from the walls, the floor bowing, and some of the floor tiles lifting to release sprouting plants and mists.

Harmonica

Even though a harmonica is not represented, an attendee mentioned the picture was as unwavering like a harmonica. An attendee noted the glass harmonica in the 1800s was associated with evil, insanity, and unraveling because of its bittersweet sound.

Composition

The scene is a room, not exactly rectangular but like a pathway, opening toward us (single-point perspective).

Empty seat at the back

An empty wooden chair sits against the back wall, set behind the other treble clef instrument beside another wall muse. We were talking about how it could be arranged for somebody to sit and engage with the instrument, collaborating with its wall muse. That instrument is reversed: if a person were sitting in that chair, they would view it in the same way as the central figure in the foreground does, with the treble clef to the left.

The muses

The two wall muses

We called them “muses” – a being appears from the left-side wall collaborating with the protagonist on the treble clef instrument. The other muse appears from the right-side wall further away and is also holding or moving an object through the topmost line (F note line) of the instrument.

These beings seem to be emerging from layers of torn paper – an attendee said they’re like peeling wallpapers.

Another muse: A face in the back wall

An attendee noted that a third muse can be perceived in the upper part of the back wall (the room has a high ceiling), where two identical windows with arched tops look like a pair of eyes. Beneath the pair of windows is a bed with an arched wooden canopy and a rounded wooden base like a boat’s hull. Together, they give it a pointed oval shape. An attendee said it looks like a cat bed. The bed is high up, above a tall bookcase/cabinet. Because of its shape plus the red drape hanging from the canopy and the red bed cover, it almost looks like a mouth. So, the two windows can be seen as eyes, and the bed beneath them a mouth.

The protagonist – the central figure

Does this person represent Remedios Varo? (I’ll refer to this being as “they” here.) They seem androgynous and wear a fashion-forward (or timeless) jacket and pants.

It’s unknown whether this person is listening. Their left hand rests on the table, holding down papers that seem to have nothing on them. An attendee wondered if the papers were braille, read by the protagonist’s fingers as instructions on how to engage with the instrument. The right hand is holding on an object on the G line of the treble clef instrument, or turning it like a knob. The left foot is positioned like it’s pressing a piano pedal, but there isn’t one. The floor seems animated, like it could be vibrating, giving sensory feedback to the protagonist.

Collaboration

The protagonist is collaborating with the wall muse, co-creating something. It also seems like they’re playing chess or the Connect Four game (a vertically placed game board between two players, in which they insert circular tokens).

There’s a feeling of alchemy in the scene, like something is being made.

The treble clef instruments

The picture has two treble clef instruments: In the foreground being engaged by the protagonist and the wall muse closest to the foreground, and another towards the back engaged by the other wall muse alone.

This instrument or tool’s treble clef has a narrow bell coming from its tail and a long mouthpiece coming from the other end, with the curl around the G line.

Attendees mentioned it looks like an abacus used in ancient accounting where people slide circular beads in narrow rods as well as Pro Tools in which audio engineers would move and twist knobs to make music. We were wondering if there were any musical instruments like this when the picture was painted in 1956.

The objects held by the rods making up the staff lines include a leaf, glass-like gems, a turnip (the root vegetable), and an unknown organic object, possibly an animal tongue. The wall muse engaging with the protagonist is holding or turning this mysterious organic object pierced through the topmost line of the Treble clef, the F line.

The treble clef instrument closest to the foreground also has two paper notes with math symbols, one of them indicating pi (3.14.15), pierced through the B and D lines.

Treble clef (formerly G) and G note

A musician in the group told us that the treble clef evolved from the letter G. The protagonist holds onto an organic rock-like object on the G line of the scale.

This is one of many delightful details of the picture.
